Certifications have been one part of my technical education, alongside professional operations, hands-on practice, vendor training, and the responsibility of teaching difficult material to other people. I value them as evidence of what I studied and worked to understand at a particular point in my career, but I don't consider a credential a substitute for being able to apply that knowledge.

My security career has taken me through threat emulation, threat hunting, and technical education. The subjects covered by the certifications I've held connect to that work, including security fundamentals, incident handling, analysis, and broader security practices.

## Certifications Previously Held

Throughout my cybersecurity career, I've earned and previously held the following professional certifications:

- **GIAC Security Essentials (GSEC)**
- **GIAC Certified Incident Handler (GCIH)**
- **CompTIA Cybersecurity Analyst (CySA+)**
- **CompTIA Advanced Security Practitioner (CASP+), now SecurityX**

I intentionally describe these as **previously held**, rather than implying they are all currently active. CASP+ and SecurityX refer to the same certification lineage, not two separate credentials.

## Continuing to Learn

For me, professional development has never been confined to examinations. Much of my understanding has come through doing the work, explaining it, identifying where my explanation falls short, and going back to learn more.

I'm open to obtaining or renewing professional certifications as required for future roles, responsibilities, or opportunities. What interests me most is whether a qualification supports the knowledge and capability needed for the work, not simply how many acronyms can appear after a name.

The fuller picture of my development is in [my professional experience](../experience/) and [technical capabilities](../capabilities/).
