I believe that understanding begins with curiosity, develops through reasoning, and improves through reflection.

I don't believe that knowing something means being incapable of questioning it. Quite the opposite. The more important an idea is, the more willing I should be to examine it.

My philosophy isn't intended to be a collection of unquestionable truths. It's a developing understanding of reality, knowledge, freedom, responsibility, and what it means to live thoughtfully.

## 01. Curiosity & Inquiry

I believe curiosity is one of our most valuable capabilities.

The willingness to ask questions allows us to challenge assumptions, discover possibilities, and recognize things we might otherwise overlook.

I don't believe inherited beliefs should be accepted simply because they're familiar, popular, or supported by authority.

Ideas should be open to examination, including my own.

I enjoy exploring questions that don't have obvious answers, but I also recognize that not every question has an answer we can currently establish.

Sometimes the most meaningful discovery is recognizing what we don't yet understand.

**I would rather continue questioning than become comfortable with an explanation that cannot withstand scrutiny.**

## 02. Truth & Evidence

I believe objective reality exists independently of our perceptions, interpretations, and beliefs.

What we believe about reality, however, is not necessarily reality itself.

Our understanding is developed through observation, evidence, experience, reasoning, and the willingness to reconsider our conclusions.

I try to distinguish between what I have observed, what I can derive from those observations, and what I infer might be true.

I also recognize three important states of knowledge: **True, False, and Unknown**.

Unknown is not an embarrassment or a failure. It is an honest acknowledgment that the available evidence does not yet justify a conclusion.

I value truth over the appearance of certainty.

**Confidence should be earned through evidence, not performed through conviction.**

## 03. Reasoning & Reflection

I'm interested not only in what I think, but in how I arrive at my understanding.

I distinguish between intuition, thinking, reasoning, and reflection.

Intuition can bring an idea to my attention. Thinking allows me to explore it. Reasoning helps me examine its consistency and supporting evidence. Reflection allows me to reconsider the process itself.

For me, reflection is essential because even a carefully reasoned conclusion can depend on incomplete information, incorrect assumptions, or an inadequate model.

I want to be capable of recognizing those limitations.

Changing my mind in response to better evidence is not a defeat. It is evidence that learning has occurred.

**Understanding should evolve as the evidence evolves.**

## 04. Freedom & Autonomy

I believe people deserve the freedom to think independently, question ideas, develop their own understanding, and make meaningful decisions about their lives.

Personal autonomy includes the ability to explore, disagree, make mistakes, and learn from experience.

I believe that freedom should be respected rather than unnecessarily constrained.

That respect extends beyond my own autonomy. Other people possess the same inherent worth and deserve the same dignity.

Freedom is not unlimited permission to harm others. It exists alongside responsibilities and should be balanced with the protection of people from undue harm.

I value independence of thought because understanding that has been freely developed is fundamentally different from an opinion accepted through coercion.

## 05. Ethics & Responsibility

I believe every person has equal inherent worth.

How we treat people matters, including how we exercise power, make decisions, respond to mistakes, and address harm.

I value honesty, compassion, fairness, accountability, and proportionality.

I believe authority should be exercised as a responsibility rather than treated as an entitlement.

When harm occurs, I believe our response should consider not only accountability but also repair, rehabilitation, and the prevention of future harm.

I also believe that helping others is meaningful, but service should not require abandoning responsibility for oneself.

Knowledge and capability create opportunities to contribute, but they also create obligations to consider the consequences of our actions.

**What we can do and what we should do are different questions.**

---

## A Philosophy That Continues to Evolve

I don't expect to reach a point where I've finished learning, questioning, or refining my understanding.

Some beliefs may become stronger through examination. Others may change or disappear when confronted with better evidence.

That is a process I want to remain open to throughout my life.

I want to understand reality as accurately as I can, exercise my freedom responsibly, treat others with dignity, and use what I learn to create something worthwhile.

**I don't need to have every answer. I need to remain willing to learn.**
