I've never been particularly good at limiting myself to one area of interest. When something catches my attention, I want to understand how it works, what makes it possible, how it connects to other things, and what I might be able to do with it.

I tend to approach the world as a **Thinker, Learner, Explorer, and Creator**. These aren't separate personalities. They're different ways I engage with things that interest me.

## 01. Making, Tinkering & Inventing

I enjoy building things, taking things apart, experimenting with technology, and finding unusual ways to combine existing capabilities into something new.

I'm particularly interested in modular electronics, handheld computers, cyberdecks, sensors, radios, automation, and unconventional hardware interfaces. I like systems that can be extended, modified, and repurposed rather than treated as fixed products.

My interests also extend into home laboratories, software tools, and connected systems.

For me, making isn't exclusively about producing something useful. It's a way of learning. An idea becomes much more interesting when I can interact with it, experiment with it, discover its limitations, and improve it.

**Building is one way I think with my hands.**

## 02. Philosophy, Logic & Mathematics

I'm fascinated by the foundations of things.

What makes something distinct? How do relationships emerge? What establishes boundaries? How do we know something is true? What separates an observation from an interpretation?

These questions have led me into philosophy, ontology, epistemology, logic, mathematics, and systems thinking.

I enjoy examining concepts that people often take for granted, breaking them into their underlying components, and exploring how those components relate.

I'm especially interested in developing conceptual models that help explain complex subjects without sacrificing their underlying complexity.

I don't assume that every model I develop is correct. The process of examining, challenging, and refining an idea is part of what makes the exploration worthwhile.

## 03. Writing, Learning & Knowledge Creation

I enjoy learning, but I also enjoy figuring out how knowledge can be organized, explained, and shared.

Writing is one of the ways I develop my understanding. It forces me to articulate what I think I know, confront gaps in my reasoning, and establish relationships between ideas.

My interests include books, technical manuals, educational frameworks, diagrams, reference materials, and structured knowledge systems.

I'm particularly interested in creating resources that develop understanding progressively, beginning with foundational concepts and building toward more sophisticated applications.

I believe a good explanation should make something understandable without making it misleadingly simple.

**I write to understand, and I organize knowledge so that understanding can be shared.**

## 04. Computing, AI & Experimentation

Computing fascinates me because it allows abstract ideas to become functioning systems.

I enjoy programming, automation, software architecture, artificial intelligence, and experimenting with computational capabilities.

I'm particularly drawn to systems that can make decisions, operate under constraints, adapt to changing conditions, or generate complex behavior from relatively simple rules.

I also enjoy exploring the boundaries of technology. What can a system actually do? What are its limitations? What happens when different technologies are combined?

For me, experimentation means more than making something work once. It means understanding why it works, testing whether it behaves as expected, and discovering what can be improved.

## 05. Gaming, Game Development & Worldbuilding

I've always appreciated games as interactive systems.

I enjoy exploring worlds, discovering mechanics, experimenting with possibilities, and seeing how different rules interact.

That interest naturally extends into game development and worldbuilding.

I'm especially fascinated by procedural generation, emergent behavior, evolving environments, artificial life, and simulations where interesting outcomes arise from the interaction of underlying systems.

I've explored ideas ranging from small action games to complex fantasy worlds populated by interacting characters, creatures, and environments.

Games offer an unusual combination of creativity, engineering, logic, storytelling, and experimentation.

They allow me to explore not only a world someone else imagined, but also what it takes to create one.

---

## The Common Thread

Although these interests span different disciplines, I approach them in much the same way.

I want to understand difficult things, explore their possibilities, create something from that understanding, and discover what I can learn along the way.

**Think. Learn. Explore. Create. Repeat.**
