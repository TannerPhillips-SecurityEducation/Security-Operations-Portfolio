# TSP Website: Editorial Contract

**Edition:** v0.10 · 2026-11-17

## Purpose

This is a personal website. It should introduce a curious person who happens to work in network security, engineering, and education. It is not a conventional résumé, a glossary of competencies, or an advertisement for a vendor.

## Narrative before taxonomy

A visitor should first understand why something matters, then encounter the framework that organizes it.

- Open a section with a first-person account or explanation.
- Develop a concrete idea across several connected paragraphs instead of stacking labels.
- Explain how ideas relate to one another and why those relationships matter.
- Use examples from documented experience to turn abstractions into understandable situations.
- Preserve lists, cards, or tables when they genuinely help navigation, comparison, or precise reference.
- Put long formal function enumerations, timelines, and detailed breakdowns in expandable sections only after the main explanation is visible.

Avoid multiplying prose just to fill space. A longer explanation should make a new distinction, supply an example, or connect an idea to another part of the story.

## Personal voice

Write in first person. Keep directness and precision, with curiosity and a willingness to acknowledge uncertainty. The writing should not pretend to be omniscient or claim unquestionable expertise.

Keep the approved underlying identity: Thinker, Learner, Explorer, Creator. Professional experience and personal interests are equally legitimate entry points.

Avoid overly promotional claims, self-rating scales, generic corporate language, or metaphors that substitute for a technical explanation.

## Evidence and professional disclosure

The historical career milestones are 2014 Navy, 2015 90th Cyber Protection Team, 2020 Elastic Security Education, and 2025 Corelight Security Education.

- Navy: roughly two years in Cyber Threat Emulation, then Cyber Threat Hunting. Do not disclose specific operations.
- Elastic: Education was primary, curriculum development secondary. The five-day historical sequence included Wireshark/tcpdump, Suricata, Zeek/Linux, Elastic/search, then a guided hunt with CTFd and Kibana. Do not claim sole authorship or imply the historical curriculum is still current.
- Corelight: Education and Creation are both primary. Functions and learning outcomes remain consistent across all site sections. Do not expose confidential customer or training details.
- Certifications: previously held; never implicitly current.
- Projects: manual curation and publicly inspectable outcomes only.
- Publications: intentionally released editions only.
- Knowledge: the separate Security-Education repository is authoritative. Preserve draft versus reviewed versus completed distinctions; links are not a formally approved semantic graph.

## Privacy

Never publish personal location, private relationships, private photographs, personal email, phone numbers, private contact information, medical details, or employer-confidential operations. Public connection channels are exclusively the owner-approved LinkedIn and GitHub URLs.

## Data and design

The canonical public introduction is real `src/data/TSP.yaml`. Its raw endpoint, full copy, and downloadable contents must stay valid and identical. Explanatory sections may grow without turning the file into a fabricated terminal display.

Keep the approved Obsidian Prism palette, TSP monogram, modern editorial typography, responsive reading width, focus states, and reduced-motion behavior. Readability takes precedence over decoration.

## Quality review

For each new page or revision ask:

1. Can a reader understand what this part of the story is about without opening a collapsible section?
2. Does each new paragraph add context, evidence, a meaningful distinction, or a consequence?
3. Are claims grounded, correctly scoped, and non-confidential?
4. Can a reader move naturally to the next related section?
5. Are categories and technical labels subordinate to the explanation rather than the entire explanation?

This contract is editorial guidance, not a new knowledge ontology or assessment taxonomy.
